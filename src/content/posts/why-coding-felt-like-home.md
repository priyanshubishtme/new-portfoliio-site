Most subjects in school felt like a test of memory. You study, you repeat, you forget. Computer science was different — it was the first subject where I could actually _make_ something. Not memorize a formula, but sit down and build.

In classes 11 and 12, that process pulled me in. When something didn't work, I didn't feel lost — I felt curious. The debugging was the learning. By the end of senior secondary, I knew what I wanted: to make things, not just study them.

That clarity is rare when you're sixteen or seventeen. I'm grateful I found it early enough to chase it.
