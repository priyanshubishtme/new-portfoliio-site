For a long time, I was the quiet one in the room. Not shy exactly — just careful. I would rehearse what I wanted to say so many times in my head that by the time I was ready, the moment had passed.

The first time I walked on stage to introduce myself, I wasn't prepared. Someone asked who hadn't spoken yet, and before I could overthink it, I stood up. My voice wasn't steady. My points weren't polished. But I said something, and the room didn't fall apart.

That small, unremarkable moment changed how I think about speaking. It's not about being smooth or confident. It's about showing up before you're ready, and trusting that the practice comes after the first step, not before it.
