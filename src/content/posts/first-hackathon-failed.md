Our team of four walked into our first hackathon with a half-formed idea and too much confidence. We spent the night building a project that, by morning, barely worked. The demo was rough. We didn't place.

But on the way home, something shifted. We talked about what went wrong — not with blame, but with curiosity. The kind of post-mortem you can only have when you've actually tried something and watched it break.

That hackathon taught me that shipping something imperfect beats not shipping at all. The failure wasn't the end. It was the beginning of everything that came after: better projects, clearer thinking, and the courage to show up again.
